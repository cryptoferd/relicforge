How Relic Forge R12-v2 Works

Relic Forge is doing something fairly technical under the hood, but the creator experience should not feel that way. You build the collection in Studio, choose how minting and reveal should work, and Relic Forge turns those choices into a small set of purpose-built onchain contracts.

This page explains what those contracts do, why the system is split up the way it is, and what actually happens after a collector clicks mint.

Current environment: R12-v2 is certified preproduction on Ethereum Sepolia. Mainnet launch remains disabled. Creator UI R1 is aligned to the certified V2 contract stack, while the V2 collector mint page is intentionally held back for its own rewrite.

The 30-second version

Your collection is its own permanent onchain object

Each launch gets lightweight Collection, ProjectData, and MintPhases clones. They are bound to the implementation selected by the Factory when they are created; there is no proxy-admin upgrade switch hiding behind them.

Artwork and sale rules are separated

ProjectData holds the immutable collection content. MintPhases controls sale stages, allowlists, wallet limits, prices, schedules, and the master mint switch. The Collection handles NFTs, creator controls, proceeds, and reveal state.

You choose between two reveal experiences

Deferred Reveal mints hidden NFTs first and reveals them later with one verified random word. Forge Reveal accepts reservations first, groups them into small batches, then randomizes and settles those batches later.

Randomness funding is built into the collection

Reveal costs are paid from the collection's hopper first. If the hopper is short, the shared Reserve can cover only the exact bounded shortfall. The system never gives a randomness request an unlimited spending blank check.

What gets created when you launch

Mint stages: MintPhases is the gatekeeper

In V2, the Collection no longer owns the sale-stage table. RelicMintPhasesV2 decides whether a collector is allowed to mint and how much that mint costs before the Collection accepts it.

A stage can have its own price, start and end time, stage supply, max-per-wallet limit, access type, Merkle root, priority, and enabled state. The master Minting switch also lives there. This means Creator Dashboard actions such as “Pause Mint,” stage editing, and wallet limits talk to MintPhases directly.

For an Approved Wallet stage, the proof is bound to the chain, collection, phase, payer, and allowance:

keccak256(abi.encode(block.chainid, collection, phaseId, payer, allowance))

Two reveal experiences

CONTRACT VALUE 0
Deferred Reveal

The NFT is minted immediately, but it shows the forging placeholder. Later, the creator requests one delayed reveal. One verified Chainlink word permanently determines the collection-wide assignment permutation for the minted supply.

There is no creator reroll. If the reveal happens before the collection sells out, future mints automatically and irreversibly switch to Forge Reveal so already-used recipes cannot be drawn again.

CONTRACT VALUE 1
Forge Reveal

A successful collector transaction creates a reservation first. It does not call Chainlink and may not emit the final NFT Transfer events yet.

Reservations fill batches of up to 20 NFTs. Full batches lock automatically; a partial batch can be locked after the configured batch window. Randomness is then requested, delivered, and the batch is settled into actual NFTs and unique recipe assignments.

Why randomness is split into request, replay, and settle

The goal is to keep the Chainlink callback deliberately small and predictable. Chainlink proves the random word; the adapter stores that exact word and returns. Relic Forge then performs collection delivery in a separate permissionless transaction.

1
RequestFor Forge, anyone can call requestRandomnessForBatch(batchId) after a batch is locked. Creator tooling uses an explicit 1,500,000 transaction gas limit.
2
Chainlink callbackThe V2 adapter stores the exact verified word only. It does not try to execute the Collection inside the upstream callback.
3
ReplayAnyone can call replayFulfillment(localRequestId). Creator tooling uses an explicit 1,000,000 gas limit.
4
Post-checkAfter replay mines, the UI checks deliveredForLocalRequest(localRequestId). A transaction receipt by itself is not treated as proof that delivery actually reached the Collection.
5
SettlesettleReady(maxTokens) turns ready Forge reservations into NFTs and assigns unique recipes in deterministic batch order.

Where the ETH goes

Relic Forge keeps creator sale proceeds and reveal funding separate. That distinction matters.

Creator Covers Platform Fee

The launch fee is quoted from the configured USD rate and maximum supply. The required ETH is paid when the collection is created and initializes the collection's reveal hopper. Collectors do not pay a Relic Forge per-mint platform fee.

Collector Covers Platform Fee

The active collection fee is quoted during minting. When the oracle is healthy and the fee is active, that amount is added to the collection hopper. If the oracle is unhealthy, the current minter-supported path fails open to a $0 platform fee instead of blocking the collector mint.

The hopper is the collection's first source of randomness funding. If a request costs more than the hopper currently holds, the Collection may ask the shared RelicForgeReserveV2 for only the exact bounded shortfall. The request is rejected if the live randomness quote exceeds the creator's configured maxRandomnessCostPerBatchWei ceiling.

Reserve ETH protecting reveal obligations is not automatically platform revenue. Only surplus above the Reserve's required safety level can be released to the Reserve revenue treasury.

Creator earnings are separate from reveal funding

In Deferred mode, collector sale proceeds become accrued creator proceeds after the hidden NFT mint succeeds. In Forge mode, collector sale value waits in creatorEscrow while the reservation is pending, then moves to accruedCreatorProceeds when that reservation settles.

Calling withdraw() sends creator proceeds to the configured payoutReceiver. The caller cannot redirect the withdrawal to a different wallet.

Creator/team mint is still fee-aware

creatorMint(address,uint32) is payable in R12-v2. In Collector Covers Platform Fee mode, MintPhases exposes creatorTeamFeeQuote(quantity). If the collection fee is active and the oracle is healthy, the creator/team mint pays approximately half of the current per-NFT platform fee. Studio quotes that amount before sending the mint.

Your artwork and metadata remain onchain

R12-v2 keeps the existing ProjectData/renderer design: artwork and DNA are stored in code-backed shards, and the Collection points to the immutable ProjectData clone. Studio keeps individual shards comfortably below the EVM code-size ceiling with its own safety margin.

The immutable renderer supports raw SVG fragments, PNG, JPEG, and WEBP encodings. Raw GIF is not a native immutable renderer encoding, so animated artwork must be converted to a supported onchain representation before compile.

The NFT surface includes ERC-165, ERC-721, ERC-721 Metadata, ERC-2981 royalties, ERC-4906 metadata refresh signaling, ERC-173 ownership, and ERC-7572 contract metadata. It provides totalSupply() for indexer compatibility but intentionally does not claim ERC-721 Enumerable.

FeePolicy treasury and Reserve revenue treasury are different destinations with different authority. In the hardened FeePolicy, setTreasury(address) only proposes a new treasury. The target wallet must accept onchain before the treasury actually changes.

What Creator UI R1 protects you from

  • Launching on the wrong network: R1 requires Ethereum Sepolia for the certified preproduction flow.
  • Launching through an empty/non-contract Factory address.
  • Reveal-mode inversion: the V2 mapping is direct — Deferred = 0, Forge = 1.
  • Old saved projects silently changing reveal behavior: schema migration preserves their original choice.
  • Under-gassed request/replay actions: the UI applies the certified conservative gas limits.
  • Treating a mined replay as automatically successful: the UI verifies actual delivery afterward.
  • Publishing the old collector page against a V2 collection.
  • Accidentally treating Mainnet as live: Mainnet configuration remains non-deployed with launch disabled.

What R1 intentionally does not do yet

The old V1 collector mint page assumes a successful mint transaction immediately produces final ERC-721 Transfer events and that sale stages live on the Collection. Neither assumption is safe for R12-v2 Forge Reveal.

For that reason, R12-v2 public collector mint-page publishing is intentionally disabled in Creator UI R1. A dedicated V2 collector-page rewrite is required before V2 collections are exposed through the public mint experience.

Security statement: the R12-v2 contract stack has passed Relic Forge's internal automated and live Sepolia certification work, but that is evidence rather than a guarantee. This work is not a professional audit. Mainnet deployment and activation remain a separate, disabled phase.
